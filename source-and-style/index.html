Toy Penguin by Claire Falla

This toy penguin was made by New Zealand textile artist Claire Falla, who produced a range of New Zealand native animals between 1960 - 1980. The toys were early examples of their type and were made and sold in New Zealand in response to the large number of Australian koalas and kangaroos available and the lack of New Zealand wildlife.

About Claire Falla

Claire was the daughter of the Dominion Museum's former director Dr Robert Falla, an ornithologist, and Molly Falla, the author of several books on birds. Claire was deeply concerned about the welfare of New Zealand native animals and made these toys as replicas in the hope that children would gain knowledge, understanding, and affection for them.

Claire was actively involved in the welfare of animals as well — the Falla household regularly nursed sick penguins, kiwi, and on one occasion a kea.

Animal Welfare Context

Claire Falla's work was pioneering in connecting children with wildlife and conservation. Her creation of these toy animals in the 1960s-1980s was ahead of its time in terms of promoting environmental awareness and animal welfare among young people. The fact that her household actively rescued and cared for sick native birds shows the family's deep commitment to animal welfare.
